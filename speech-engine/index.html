The Amenti Speech Engine

One engine gives voice to every figure in the Library of Legends. It holds a conversation in the Amenti interface, and it reads Dracula aloud with a full cast.

01One engine, every voice

Most audiobooks have one reader. A few have a cast of actors. Amenti uses a cast of historical figures. Each figure in the Library of Legends has a voice of their own, and that same voice answers when a reader talks to them in the Amenti interface.

Both jobs use the same engine. The voice that reads Harker's journal is the voice you can question afterwards. That rules out per-book voice files and hand-tuned recordings. Each voice has to be described once, as data, and give the same result every time.

02A voice from three columns

Every figure is a row in the Library's ledger, and three columns of that row make the voice:

ColumnWhat it decides
GenderThe base voice, a deep male voice or a clear female voice
DialectThe accent: "Dutch, curious and plain" or "Texas Hill Country, overbearing and earthy"
VoiceThe manner: "ironic and probing", "haunted and hollow"

The engine combines these into a short spoken direction and sends it with the text. Abraham Lincoln and Julius Caesar share a base voice. Their dialect and manner make them two different men. That's how the system is meant to work.

// composed for Van Helsing, as cast voice male base style Read clearly, in a measured, dignified tone. Accent and dialect: Dutch, curious and plain. Voice character: ironic and probing. Speak at a brisk, lively, natural pace.

03Casting Dracula

A role in a book is played by a figure from the Library, not by a voice preset. The casting is part of the reading. A Victorian naturalist far from home reads Jonathan Harker's journal. A Wallachian prince speaks for the Count. Stoker narrates his own novel.

04The cue sheet

Before anything is spoken, each chapter is cut into a cue sheet. Each line of Stoker's text goes to the character who speaks it. "she said" stays with the narrator, and the quoted words go to Lucy. Every attribution is checked by hand.

{ "n": 95, "role": "Lucy Westenra", "text": "“Now,”" } { "n": 96, "role": "Dr Seward", "text": "she said," } { "n": 97, "role": "Lucy Westenra", "text": "“you must stay here. I shall leave this door open…”" }

The same cue numbers place the paintings, sounds and music. A painting enters on cue 97, at a chosen phrase, and the sound of a telegraph lands on the word it belongs to. The voice is what everything else is timed to.

Not one word of Stoker's text is rewritten. The engine speaks the novel exactly as it was published in 1897.

05Two registers

The engine speaks in two ways, and they are kept apart on purpose.

RecitalConversation
WhereBooks, reading rooms, the podcastThe Amenti terminal
TextFixed: the author's wordsNew every turn
ToneLocked once castChanges with the moment: warm, cool, sharp, grave, danger, humour
ArchiveRendered once, kept for goodNever archived

A recital can be stored because its words never change. A conversation can be expressive because nothing is stored. Mixing the two would lose both.

06The archive

Recital text is split into measures of up to 320 characters at sentence boundaries. Each measure is rendered once and filed under a fingerprint of everything that shaped it:

key = sha256( model + voice + style + text )

The second time anyone plays that line, it comes from the archive and is not rendered again. Because the key covers the whole recipe, the archive can't serve a stale voice. Change the accent and the key changes with it.

The key also brings a rule. The measure boundaries and the style wording are part of every key, so they are frozen. A fixed test passage is sent through the engine every six hours to check that they haven't drifted.

07Covering the wait

Measured on the live service, a fresh render takes about six seconds and an archived one about two:

6.1 sMedian fresh render
1.9 sMedian from the archive
3.2×Archive speed-up

Six seconds of silence after pressing a button feels broken. In conversation, the engine opens with a short first phrase to start sooner. The interface fills the gap the way a telephone does: an announcement, a tone, then a ring that stops on the figure's first sound.

08Listening

The engine listens too. When you talk with a figure, your voice is captured as 16 kHz mono speech. It decides you've finished after 1.2 seconds of silence, and it keeps the half-second before you began so your first syllable isn't lost. The microphone closes itself after 45 seconds with no speech, and no turn can run past five minutes.

It also judges the room before it transcribes. If a dog barks, the figure may notice it. Words overheard from another room are never written down.

Notice, do not record.

09Underneath

Library ledger→Voice composed→Measures of 320→Archive lookup→Speech model→Archive→Your speaker

The speech itself comes from Google's Gemini text-to-speech model. It runs through a Cloudflare Worker, and rendered audio is kept in Cloudflare R2 storage. The composing, measuring, archiving, casting and timing are Amenti's own, and they live in one place: one chunker, one style composition, one archive.
